## Rulings taken from the specification (engineering; each recorded in `docs/decisions.md` in the step that needs it)

- `ivreg` and `first_stage` live in `regress/src/iv.rs` (section 9.1's layout); `ar_test` beside them. An IV fit is an estimator of section 8.3, point 2: its design holds the exogenous and endogenous regressors and the instruments, its fit replaces each endogenous column by its first-stage fitted values and solves the second stage by the kernel's QR, its scores are the structural residuals times the fitted regressors and its bread their cross-product inverse, so the variance layer gives classical, HC1, HC2, HC3 and clustered errors unchanged, with the degrees of freedom of the ruling of 0.6.0. Under `fe:` the outcome, the regressors and the instruments are projected out together, then the two stages run on what remains.
- Self-checks after an IV fit (section 11.1): the structural residuals orthogonal to the instruments and the exogenous regressors, a symmetric positive semi-definite variance matrix, converged demeaning; a model with exogenous "instruments" equal to its regressors reproduces `reg` to the bit, which a property test holds.
- The weak-instrument statistics are computed by the engine's kernels: the Cragg–Donald statistic as the smallest eigenvalue of the concentration matrix by the kernel's symmetric eigenvalue routine (new in `zeff-kernels`, pure Rust, proved identical on the four targets), the Kleibergen–Paap rk statistic by the kernel's SVD, the effective F by Montiel Olea and Pflueger's formula on the robust first-stage variance. All are of the closed-form class (1e-6 relative). The Anderson–Rubin set is solved exactly as a quadratic inequality where it is one (one endogenous regressor; classical errors, or robust and clustered errors with one excluded instrument), and by a bracketed search over the statistic's roots otherwise, to 1e-10 of the scale of the estimate; its shape (interval, two rays, the whole line, empty) is part of the result and of the snapshot.
- Codes the text gives are used as given: Z0504 (an instrumented variable missing from the formula, with the example of section 11), Z0505 (a variable on both sides of `instruments:`), Z0804 (under-identification), each with its row in the table of section 11 (the text describes all three and the table lacks their rows: added at step 9 and logged, as the codes numbered while the engine was built have been). Conditions the text describes without a number receive provisional codes as before. A statistical warning for weak instruments (an F under 10, or an effective F under its critical value) is a new W1 code with its row, its explained page and its snapshot.
- Leverage under `fe:` (the promise of Z0410 and Z0304): with one absorbed effect an observation's leverage in the full design is the leverage from the demeaned columns plus one over the size of its group, exactly; `se: hc2`, `hc3` and `influence` are opened for one effect, for `reg` and `ivreg` alike; with several effects they stay refused and the message names 1.1, where two-way clustering arrives, and the known-issues entry says so.
- `zeff explain <code>` prints the page `zeff help <code>` prints, with `--format json`; alone, it lists the codes by family. The language server's diagnostics carry the code and a `codeDescription` whose link the editor opens through a request the server answers (`zeff/explain`), since no public website exists before 1.0 (the owner's ruling); Zemp reads the same page through `zeff-web`.
- `zeff report-bug` writes one file the user reads, in the project's `.zeff/reports/`, never sends anything, and includes no data unless `--include-data` is written; the statements a failing statement depends on are found from the typed IR's bindings (a dependency graph built at the step and kept for the shrinker); shrinking removes rows and statements by halves while the failure persists (the same code and the same message), bounded in time and deterministic; `--synthetic` replaces the values of the shrunk rows while the failure persists. Templates of the four kinds of issue go in `.github/ISSUE_TEMPLATE/`; the shrunk reproducer of every fixed bug joins the conformance suite under `regressions/`, the three faults of 0.7.0's close first.
- The amended help contract is enforced by the registry's build check on every page of a function released at 0.8.0 or later and on every page already revised; the pages not yet revised are listed in one file of the registry crate, each line removed as its page is revised, and the file must be empty at 0.10.0. A caption is the first line of an example, written as a comment. "Coming from R or Stata" has its equivalents generated from the catalogue's `equivalent` column and its differences written by hand, in the project's own words (the rule of `docs/decisions.md`: nothing copied from another program's documentation).
- A function that a merge or a rename removes (`days_between`, `months_between`, `add_days`, `add_months`, `quantile_group`, `fill_forward`, `running_sum`, `running_product`, `row_mean`, `row_sum`) is refused by the checker with a diagnostic that names the new call and the rewrite, under one code chosen at the first such step (a row in section 11's table, logged); the catalogue rows go, the new rows come, and the counts of both volumes follow at each step. The merged `fill_missing` lives in `data`; whether its body is Rust or Zeff is decided at the step by what the forward fill needs.
- The breaking changes of this release are #108, #130, the two renames of #96 and the six merges of #97 and #99, each listed in the notes with the rewrite it needs. Not pulled forward: `liml` and GMM (after 1.0), the CLR test, the exact intervals of effect sizes (#93, 1.9), `each(...)` over tests (#80, 0.11.0), `games_howell` (#85, 0.9.0), the launcher, `zeff doctor` and `zeff audit` (0.9.0), the run record and the caches (0.11.0), Zemp's dialogs (no edition has them, #125; the "dialog and code parity" row of the testing handbook therefore has nothing to test and the close says so).
- The catalogue row of each function is marked implemented in the step that ships it; the counts of section 8.2 do not change (the three functions are counted).
