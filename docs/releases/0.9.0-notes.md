# Zeff 0.9.0 — Binary outcomes

Released 26 November 2026. The ninth release of the Zeff engine is the one for binary outcomes: `glm`
fits a logistic or a probit regression by maximum likelihood, with robust and clustered
errors and weights; `margins` turns any fit of `reg` or `glm` into effects and predictions on
the scale the reader thinks in, with delta-method standard errors; `curve` and `confusion` say
how well a binary fit tells its two outcomes apart. Beside them, the release splits the `zeff`
command into a small launcher and versioned engines that are installed from signed archives,
which is what patch releases need from their first day, and gives each engine the list of its
known wrong results as data, which `zeff audit` reads a project's runs against. Seven functions
are new, each with its complete help page: `glm`, `curve` and `confusion` in the new package
`glm`, `margins` in `results`, `timer` in `core`, `n_chars` in `text` and `row_number` in
`data`. What the release unlocks: binary-outcome studies, and patch releases (0.9.1 and after).

## What shipped

**Logistic and probit regression.** `data.glm(y ~ x, family: binomial)` fits a logistic
regression of an outcome of 0 and 1, or a Bool; `link: probit` gives the probit. `family:` is
required, so that the one function can take other families later without a change of meaning.
The fit is by Fisher scoring, started as R starts it, with a step halved when it would lower
the likelihood; the iterations are counted under the table. The table is a z table: estimates,
standard errors, z, p-values and intervals from the normal distribution, and under it the
log-likelihood, AIC, McFadden's R², the deviance and the null deviance. `se:` takes
`classical`, `robust` (HC1), `hc2` and `hc3`, and `cluster:` clustered errors (CR1), from the
same variance layer as `reg`. `weights:` are frequency or sampling weights; sampling weights
give a pseudo-likelihood with robust errors, and analytic weights are refused, since the
variance of a 0 or 1 is fixed by its mean.

**Separation, and the fit's checks on itself.** Before a fit, a linear program looks for a
regressor, or a combination of regressors, that splits the outcomes perfectly; such a model
has no maximum, and the fit stops with Z0803 naming what separates: "`dose` perfectly predicts
`cured` (separation)". A fit that converges with fitted probabilities numerically 0 or 1 is
warned of. After every fit the engine checks that the log-likelihood never fell, that the
score is zero at the maximum and that the variance matrix is sound.

**The fit read like any other.** `coef`, `vcov`, `std_errors`, `ci`, `p_values`, `summary`,
`fit_measures`, `fitted`, `residuals` (response, Pearson, deviance, working), `predict` on the
scale of the probability or of the link, `test` (Wald), `compare` (the likelihood-ratio test
of two nested fits on the same rows), `derive`, `design`, `report` and `export` beside fits of
`reg`, and `coef_plot`. `fit.summary(exponentiate: true)` reads a logit as odds ratios, each
with its interval; the option came into this release by the owner's decision of 23 November.

**Marginal effects.** `fit.margins(educ)` gives the average effect of a variable on the
outcome through every term it enters: after `y ~ exper + exper^2` the slope in `exper` is
taken through both terms. A number gives a slope; a Bool, a 0/1 variable or a category gives
the difference between its values. `at: {exper: 10}` holds a variable at a value, and a list
of values gives the result at each; `type: mem` takes the effects at the means; with no
variable named the result is the adjusted prediction. After `glm` everything is on the scale
of the probability. Standard errors are by the delta method from the fit's own variance,
classical, robust or clustered. A margins result prints as a table, stands in `report` beside
fits, and is read by `estimates`, `std_errors`, `ci` and `p_values`.

**How well a binary fit classifies.** `fit.curve(type: roc)` gives the ROC curve at every
distinct fitted probability and the area under it, computed exactly; `plot()` draws it,
`statistic()` gives the area and `estimates()` the points. `fit.confusion(cutoff: 0.5)` gives
the table of observed against predicted outcomes with sensitivity, specificity, accuracy and
the two predictive values. Both take `newdata:`, for rows the fit did not see.

**`timer`.** `timer(expr)` runs an expression, returns its value and notes the time it took.

**The launcher and the engines.** The `zeff` command is now two things: a small launcher,
which reads a project's `zeff.toml`, finds the engine it pins and hands the command over, and
engines, one for each version, kept side by side in a store. `zeff engine list`, `install`,
`remove` and `default` manage the store; `zeff engine use <version>` runs a project on its
pinned engine and on another and says in sentences what differs before the pin is changed;
`zeff doctor` reports the state of an installation with the command that mends each problem;
`zeff version --verbose` says what a build is. An engine is installed from an archive that is
listed in a signed file of checksums; the launcher checks the signature and the checksum
before it unpacks anything, and refuses a file that is not the one published (Z0711). The
launcher trusts one key, the publisher's, built into it (minisign key id DB06ECEA9967641F),
and the archives of this release are signed with it. Before 1.0 nothing is served from a
public address: an engine is installed from a downloaded archive with
`zeff engine install --from <file>`, or from a mirror. Archives are built for Linux on x86-64
and ARM64 and for macOS on ARM64.

**Known wrong results, as data.** Each engine carries the list of the wrong results known
when it was built: the functions and options each affects, the first version affected and the
version that corrects it. `zeff run` keeps a small record of each run, and `zeff audit` reads
a project's runs against the newest list and says plainly which runs an issue touches. The list of
0.9.0 has three entries; the third was found and corrected at this release (see "A wrong
result corrected").

**For an interface.** What the editor of Zemp Lite asked of the engine: the plots of a run
kept by the browser build and laid out again at another size and theme without a new run;
each statement's result with its type, its dimensions and what it depends on, and its print
made again at another width; an error's chain of calls as fields, with the pass of a loop;
and sections in a script, a comment `// # Title`, which the language server reports as an
outline and the formatter keeps.

**Decisions due at this release, built.** Games and Howell's comparisons in `posthoc`
(`method: games_howell`); a warning repeated in the passes of a loop said once with its
count; `n_chars`, `row_number` and `row(vars, stat: n_missing)`; `first`, `last` and `x[i]`
on a column; a column guessed as whole numbers on import widened to Float when a decimal is
met further down; six smaller refusals lifted; six printed layouts mended; the messages a
reader coming from R meets for `I(...)`, `poly(...)` and a function of a later release; and a
citation file.

**NIST's certified values.** The thirty-one problems of NIST's Statistical Reference
Datasets for linear regression, univariate statistics and the analysis of variance are in the
conformance record, each with the number of digits to which every certified value is
reached.

**A tenth sample project.** `samples/binary`: union membership on the shipped wages by logit
and probit, with odds ratios, marginal effects, the two tests, the ROC curve and the
classification table in the sample and on years kept back, a separated fit caught with
`attempt`, and the table of the two fits.

**Help pages.** The pages of `data`, `datasets`, `dist` and `stats`, 84 in all, are brought to
the fuller form of 0.8.0: the commonest forms first, missing values under their own heading,
a common mistake with its real message, the equivalents in R and Stata, a caption on every
example. 61 pages remain, of `results`, `regress` and `plot`, for 0.10.0.

## Breaking changes

1. `logistic`, `probit` and `roc`, names the specification carried before this release and no
   released engine ran, are `glm(y ~ x, family: binomial)`, the same with `link: probit`, and
   `fit.curve(type: roc)`.
2. A warning repeated word for word in the passes of a loop is printed once, with the count
   of passes. No rewrite; the stored output of such a script is shorter.
3. Six printed layouts changed: a coefficient of `report` that printed as `0.000` is written
   to two significant digits with its sign; `labels:` reaches the parts of an interaction;
   degrees of freedom carry thousands separators; a facet of a number is titled `kids = 0`;
   hypotheses too long for the page have their symbols under their words; `estimates()` of a
   t-test gives the standard error. No rewrite; stored output changes in those places.
4. On import, a column guessed as whole numbers that meets a decimal further down is read as
   Float, with a note naming the row. A column given its type with `types:` is as before.
5. Under `weights:`, the note of a test counts the rows whose weight is missing and says how
   many a zero weight left out. The note of such a call changes, and no figure does.
6. `merge` with `suffixes:` drops a panel declaration whose variable the suffix renamed, with
   a note; declare the panel again on the new names.
7. A script's own function may no longer be called by a name the release adds (`glm`,
   `curve`, `confusion`, `margins`, `timer`, `n_chars`, `row_number`; Z0109).
8. The `zeff` on a reader's path is the launcher. A project's `zeff.toml` names the engine it
   runs on, and the launcher asks for that engine when it is not installed.
9. `compare` refuses two fits of different outcomes, two fits made on different data with as
   many rows, and two fits under different weights, each in its own words (Z0416), where it
   compared them (see "A wrong result corrected"). Two models fitted to one frame with the
   same weights print as before. Rewrite: fit both models to the same frame.

## A wrong result corrected

From 0.4.0 to 0.8.0 `compare` took two regressions to be on the same rows whenever their
frames had as many rows and the same places in them were used. A pair of different outcomes,
a pair fitted to different data of one size (two halves, two samples) or a pair under
different weights was compared, and the F it printed meant nothing (-8985.04 in one case).
An analysis is affected only if it compared such a pair; two models fitted to one frame with
the same `weights:` were compared rightly. It is issue 3 of the list `zeff audit` reads:
`zeff audit` in a project names any recorded run that called `compare` on an engine before
0.9.0 (runs are recorded from 0.9.0, so for earlier work the entry in
`docs/known_issues.md` is the guide).

## Known issues

As `docs/known_issues.md`, section "As of 0.9.0": `glm` for a binary outcome alone; `margins`
does not compare its rows; `curve` with the ROC curve alone; 61 help pages not yet revised;
three entries of NIST's certified values; the exact rank-sum test on samples with few ties;
three small things not mended; the separation check that gives no answer on a large design
in which every row is met with both outcomes (#226); a slope of `margins` through `floor` or
`round` where the data sit on the jumps (#227); `compare` knows the same rows by the
outcome and the weights, not by the regressors.

The reader test of the testing handbook, required from 0.8.0, was not run: no reader
outside the project has read the scripts of 0.9.0. The owner moved it on 26 November 2026
to 0.11.0 (#230), where the sheet is brought to that release and run on a Zemp Lite alpha
that carries a newer engine; a sheet of ten scripts with its key is ready for it.
