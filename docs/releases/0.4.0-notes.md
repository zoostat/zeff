# Zeff 0.4.0 — Regression

Released 30 October 2026. The fourth release of the Zeff engine is its first model: `reg`, linear
regression with classical, robust (HC1, HC2, HC3) and clustered standard errors and frequency,
analytic and sampling weights, on a model matrix built from formula values with categories,
contrasts and interactions; the result and estimator interfaces that every later model shares
(`summary`, `coef`, `vcov`, `predict`, `test`, `compare`, `derive`, `report`, `export` among
them); types and categories (`to`, `is`, `type_of`, `category`, `levels`, `base`, `label`,
`recode`); and under it the `zeff-kernels` crate, whose fixed reduction shape and blocked
tall-skinny QR give the same bits on every target. 29 functions, each with its complete help page.
The sample project `samples/regression/` is the release's analysis: a wage regression from the
import to a four-column publication table.

## What shipped

**The kernel.** `zeff-kernels` (pure Rust, no dependency, `unsafe` denied): a dense matrix, the
reductions of open decision #47 (sixteen independent accumulators combined in a fixed order, so
that a sum is the same bits whether or not the compiler vectorises it), a tall-skinny QR in row
blocks that never allocates the design, a pivoted QR with a relative tolerance, Cholesky and the
triangular solves. Every sum over observations in `zeff-numeric` moved to this shape (step 2b)
without changing a bit of any published result, as the arithmetic predicts for compensated sums.

**Types and categories.** A Category carries its order and its contrast (`treatment`, `sum`,
`helmert`, `poly`) in the Arrow metadata; `to`, `is` and `type_of` convert and inspect values;
`category` builds a categorical variable with its levels and contrast; `levels` and `base` read
and set the reference level; `label` attaches value labels and `recode` maps values or levels.

**Formulas and the model matrix.** A formula is a value: `y ~ educ + exper^2 + category(industry)`
lowers to terms (variables, powers, logarithms, interactions `a:b` and `a*b`, `interact(...)`,
categories under each contrast with columns named `var=level`, the intercept removed by `-1`),
and the model matrix follows the rules of section 7.2 of the Language Reference: rows with `NA`
dropped and counted, collinear columns dropped and reported, zero and vanishing columns dropped
with a note, polynomial terms orthonormal.

**`reg`.** Ordinary least squares in blocks over the design, with `se: classical | robust | hc2 |
hc3`, `cluster:` (CR1, with the few-clusters warning), `weights:` with `weight_type: frequency |
analytic | sampling`, and `level:`. The printed result is the summary of question 3: the title,
the N line (with the rows dropped and, under frequency weights, the sum of the weights), the
standard-error line, the table of estimates with t values, p-values and intervals, and R². The
statistical warnings W1001 to W1003 and W1006 (collinearity, few clusters, extreme weights,
driving observations) and the self-checks after every fit (orthogonal residuals, a symmetric variance)
are in place; an essentially perfect fit says so.

**Results.** Every fitted model and test implements the `Result` interface: `summary`
(`standardized:`, `fit_measures:`), `estimates` (a frame of terms), `std_errors`, `p_values`,
`ci`, `n_obs`; and every estimator `coef`, `vcov`, `fit_measures`, `predict(newdata:)`,
`residuals`, `fitted`, `r_squared`, `influence` (leverage, Cook's distance, studentized
residuals), `test` (Wald tests of linear restrictions written as `educ == exper`), `compare`
(nested fits on the same rows) and `derive` (nonlinear combinations with delta-method standard
errors, by automatic differentiation). `report` lays several fits out as a publication table
with stars, standard errors in parentheses and `stats:` rows, labelled by `labels:`; `export`
writes a result, a table or a report to Word (`.docx`) or LaTeX (`.tex`).

**Diagnostics.** 94 codes, 23 more than 0.3.0 (the regression's Z0801 to Z0803, the inference
codes Z0414 to Z0417, the design and weight codes Z0216, Z0223 and Z0224, the formula codes Z0321
and Z0322, the export code Z0716, and the warnings W0202, W0203, W0206, W0207, W0503, W0602,
W1001 to W1003 and W1006), each with a snapshot of its wording and an explained page behind
`zeff help <code>`. Z0803 names an outcome too large, or too small against its regressors, to
compute with.

**Testing.** The R reference harness gained `regression.R` (36 fits against `lm` with `sandwich`,
with an inference block of ten fits, fourteen Wald tests and the delta method through `deriv`),
`design.R`, `kernels.R` and `types.R`; the nightly job runs a truth-recovery Monte Carlo (fifty
seeds under three designs and five kinds of standard error) beside the stochastic class; a sixth
fuzz target feeds random frames, formulas and options through `reg` and every method; the weekly
job runs mutation testing in 24 shards over four crates and a coverage job; and a benchmark suite
(import, summary and mean by group, `reg`, at 10⁴, 10⁶ and 10⁷ rows) reports its times. 826
tests run on every push.

## Breaking changes

Six names became functions of `core`, which a script cannot rebind (Z0105): `to`, `is`,
`type_of`, `category`, `levels` and `base`. A 0.3.0 script with `let base = 1` or
`let levels = 5` is refused; the rewrite is to rename the binding. `let label = …` and
`let recode = …` still run, with the warning W0101 that they hide a function of `data`. No printed
result of a 0.3.0 script changes: the 81 conformance cases of 0.3.0 kept their digests through
the release, except the three of the milestone script, whose own text was repaired (see
Validation).

## Known issues

See `docs/known_issues.md`. New at this release: a regression refuses an outcome above 1e150 in
size, or so small against its regressors that an estimate falls below 2.2e-308, and drops a
regressor beyond those sizes with a note; weights more than 1e150 apart and frequency weights
whose total exceeds 2^53 are refused; `predict` gives no standard error of prediction; a test's
result has no interval; `test` reads linear restrictions only and `derive` differentiates eight
functions of one argument; `compare` needs nested fits on the same rows; `report` labels terms by
identifier; `influence` gives no Cook's distance or studentized residual with one residual degree
of freedom; an exactly perfect fit prints `Inf` t values; mutation testing of a slow package
against the whole workspace's tests times out under the weekly job's limit; the Edgeworth series
of Spearman's exact tail is pinned only by the stats package's comparison with R. Resolved at this
release: `label`, `category` and `recode` attach labels, value labels and categories from a
script. The browser bundle's figures are updated.
