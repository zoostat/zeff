# Release 0.4.0 — Regression

The row of section 14 of the Engine and Project Specification: 29 functions (`reg` without fixed effects; the result and estimator interfaces of `results`, 17 functions with `derive` and `report` among them; `r_squared` and `influence`; `interact`; types and categories with contrasts, `to`, `is`, `type_of`, `category`, `levels`, `base`; `label`, `recode`), on these engine pieces: the `zeff-kernels` crate (tall-skinny QR, Cholesky, a fixed SIMD width); the formula parser and the model-matrix rules of section 7.2 of the Language Reference, with collinearity reporting; the column metadata store and its keys (section 4.3 of the Language Reference); the estimator trait and the variance layer (classical, HC, clustered); Word and LaTeX tables. The analysis it runs end to end: a cross-sectional regression.

How to read this file: as `docs/releases/0.3.0.md`. Each step is small enough to finish and commit in one session; **[engine]** marks a hard engine piece, **[function]** a catalogued function on machinery that exists. The specification freeze (open decision #40) ended with 0.3.0: a change to the normative text is now an ordinary design decision, logged in `docs/spec_changes.md` with its date, section and reason; a design question the text does not settle still becomes an open decision with a deadline release. Three lessons of the 0.3.0 close are built in: every reference file of this release carries inputs from 1e7 to 1e12 beside its working grid (risk register, R3); every new kernel carries exact tests of its own, so that its mutants die in the kernel's suite in seconds and the weekly job is not left to the slow tests above it; and every property test is written to what doubles can give, comparing printed values or holding a tolerance, never the last bit of two routes through floating point.

## Rulings taken from the specification (engineering; each recorded in `docs/decisions.md` in the step that needs it)

- Where the pieces live, by the tiers of section 8.1: `kernel/zeff-kernels` holds the linear algebra (rule 8); `core` declares the shared values (`Formula`, `Terms`, the result value behind `RegFit`, `TestResult`) and the type functions (rule 4); `packages/tier1/results` holds the estimator trait, the variance layer, the design built from a formula (the `design` step of every estimator, which every model package needs), the result and estimator interfaces, `report` and the table writers (rule 5); `packages/tier2/regress` holds the OLS estimator, `r_squared`, `influence` and `interact`; `label` and `recode` are frame verbs of `data`. The tier check already names `results` and `regress` in their places.
- The kernels crate is pure Rust with `unsafe` denied and uses no intrinsics at this release: every reduction has the fixed lane shape of #47, so its bits do not depend on whether the compiler vectorises a loop (Rust never reorders floating-point operations), which is the substance of section 8.3's "fixed blocking and reduction order, whatever the SIMD width"; explicit 128-bit intrinsics are an optimisation for a later release if the benchmarks of #52 ask for it, and change no bit by construction. No third-party linear-algebra crate appears anywhere: R and exact rational arithmetic in Python are the references, as at 0.3.0.
- Estimation follows section 7.2 and section 13 exactly: regressors scaled to unit norm, the design factorised by Householder QR in row blocks and never by the normal equations, the pivoted QR of the small R with the 1e-7 tolerance for collinearity, dropped columns named and shown as "(omitted)", coefficients and the variance matrix unscaled afterwards, the condition number of the scaled R reported exactly (its singular values by one-sided Jacobi; R's `kappa(exact = TRUE)` is the reference) with W1001 above 1e8.
- Standard errors and degrees of freedom: `classical` σ²(X′X)⁻¹; `robust` is HC1, n/(n − k) times HC0, Stata's `robust`; `hc2` and `hc3` by leverage; `cluster:` is CR1 with Stata's small-sample adjustment G/(G − 1) · (n − 1)/(n − k); t statistics and intervals use n − k degrees of freedom for classical and HC errors and G − 1 when clustered, as Stata's `regress` and reghdfe do (section 13: "CR1 clustered errors with reghdfe's degrees-of-freedom rules"); R's `lm` and `sandwich` match the first three and the page records where R's default differs. The self-checks of section 11.1 run after every fit (residuals orthogonal to the regressors, a symmetric positive semi-definite variance matrix); a failed check is an internal error, never a result.
- Weights follow section 6 and the owner's decision of 0.2.0: frequency weights replicate rows (N is their sum, the degrees of freedom their sum less k); analytic weights are scaled to sum to n (Stata's `aweight`); sampling weights make `se:` default to `robust` and refuse `classical` (Z0410); an `NA` weight drops its row with a count, a zero weight leaves it out with a note, a negative weight is an error.
- Term names are those of section 7.2 (`(intercept)`, `educ`, `industry=Retail`, `educ:female`, `log(wage)`, `exper^2`, a level holding `=`, `:` or `+` quoted), with question 5 for the other contrasts; they are stable within an edition. Because the schema pass arrives at 0.6.0, a term name and a category level are checked when the statement runs, with the codes section 3.7 gives.
- `TestResult` arrives at this release for `test` and `compare`; the tests of `stats` reuse it at 0.7.0. `p_values()` on it gives its single p-value, as the catalogue says.
- Not pulled forward: `fe:` and `event` (0.6.0), `vif` (0.5.0), `.plot()`, `coef_plot` and the `size:` of `export` (0.7.0), `se: bootstrap` and `reps:` (1.1), `cluster:` with several variables, `margins` and `contrasts` (after 1.0), `report` skipping `Failed` values (0.5.0, with `attempt`), `ivreg` (0.8.0), `logistic` and `probit` (0.9.0). By the ruling of 0.1.0 only working options are registered, so `reg`'s usage carries no `fe:` until 0.6.0. `summary` has two homes, frames in `data` and results in `results`, chosen by the receiver's type as `quantile`'s are.
- Conditions the specification describes without a number receive provisional codes, recorded together as open decision #54 with deadline 0.5.0, as #41 to #46 were: an unknown term name, `Inf` in a model variable, an outcome without variation or a design with no column left, a `compare` of models on different rows or not nested, a nonlinear restriction given to `test`, an expression `derive` cannot differentiate, a level unseen by the fit in `predict`, `interact` outside a formula, an export extension that is not a format, a `recode` key that is no level.
- Exported files are deterministic: the `.docx` is a minimal WordprocessingML document in a zip container written by hand with the stored method and fixed timestamps (no crate added), the `.tex` a `tabular` with booktabs rules; both are hashed into the conformance report so the four targets prove them identical, and both are written only under the project folder (`output/` created as needed, Z0707 outside it; section 2).
- The reference harness stays R 4.6.0 with `renv.lock` (packages added under question 2), exact rational arithmetic in Python's `fractions` for the kernels' small systems (`tests/reference/kernels.py`, as `special.py` is the strict reference of the special functions), and the inputs from 1e7 to 1e12 in every new reference file; `predict` requires `newdata:` (its usage gives no default; `fitted()` is the in-sample case); `report`'s `stats:` defaults to `[n_obs, r_squared]` as 10.2 shows, its stars are 0.05, 0.01 and 0.001, and `labels:` renames terms; the fitted design is kept for the run under (frame, formula, rows used), so a second `reg` with another `se:` or `cluster:` reuses it (section 8.3, point 10).
- The catalogue row of each function is marked implemented in the step that ships it, as since 0.2.0.
