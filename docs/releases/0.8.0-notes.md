# Zeff 0.8.0 — Instrumental variables

Released 20 November 2026. The eighth release of the Zeff engine is the one for
instrumental-variables studies: `ivreg` fits a model by two-stage least squares, with fixed
effects, weights and the standard errors of `reg`; `first_stage` shows how strong the
instruments are (the F of the instruments, the Cragg–Donald, Kleibergen–Paap and effective F,
the partial R², the over-identification test and the test of endogeneity); and `ar_test` gives
the Anderson–Rubin test and a confidence set that stay valid when the instruments are weak.
Beside them: two commands, `zeff explain` for the page of any error or warning code and `zeff
report-bug`, which writes a report of a failure and shrinks it to a few rows; the date and time
functions a `DateTime` was missing; `variance_test`; six families of names merged into one
function with an option each; export to Typst; a new shipped dataset, `staff`, with a ninth
sample project on it; and the first third of the help pages brought to the fuller form every
page will have by 1.0. Ten functions are new, each with its complete help page: `ivreg`,
`first_stage` and `ar_test` in `regress`, six in `time`, and `variance_test` in `stats`. What
the release unlocks: instrumental-variables studies, and Zemp's alpha.

## What shipped

**Instrumental variables.** `data.ivreg(y ~ x + d, instruments: d ~ z1 + z2)` names the
instrumented regressors on the left of `instruments:` and the instruments outside the equation
on the right. It takes `fe:`, `se:` (classical, `robust`, `hc2`, `hc3`), `cluster:`, `weights:`
of the three kinds and `level:`, as `reg` does, and `first: true` prints the first-stage table
above the result. The result prints the structural table in the layout of `reg`, then a line
that says what is instrumented by what, and the weak-instrument statistic that fits the errors:
the Cragg–Donald F under classical errors, the Kleibergen–Paap F under robust or clustered ones,
with the effective F of Montiel Olea and Pflueger and its critical value where one regressor is
instrumented. A warning (W1007) says when the instruments are weak. An instrument that is also a
regressor is moved to the regressors with a note. The fit is read like any other: `coef`,
`std_errors`, `ci`, `p_values`, `summary`, `test`, `predict`, `residuals`, `design`, `export`,
`report` beside fits of `reg`, `coef_plot` and `plot`.

**The first stage.** `iv.first_stage()` prints one table for each instrumented regressor, its
partial R² (Shea's where several are instrumented), the F statistics above, the test of the
over-identifying restrictions (Sargan's under classical errors, Hansen's J otherwise, or a
sentence that a just-identified model has none) and the Durbin–Wu–Hausman test of endogeneity in
its regression form.

**The Anderson–Rubin test.** `iv.ar_test(level:)` tests that the coefficient of the instrumented
regressor is zero and prints the confidence set at `level:` in words, whatever its shape: an
interval, two rays, the whole line or the empty set. The set is unbounded exactly when the
instruments are too weak to bound the coefficient, and empty when they disagree. With several
instrumented regressors it gives the joint test and no set.

**Leverage under one absorbed effect.** `se: hc2`, `se: hc3` and `influence` now work with one
effect in `fe:`, as the messages of 0.6.0 promised; with several effects they stay refused and
the message names 1.1.

**The dataset `staff` and the sample `instruments`.** `dataset(staff)` is a synthetic firm of
2,400 employees in an encouragement design: a training course offered by lot, taken up more by
the more able, and productivity afterwards, with a true effect of 4.5 documented on its page.
The ninth sample project, `samples/instruments/`, tells the study on it: the comparison that
misleads (5.93), the offer as an instrument (4.60, with an interval that holds 4.5), the first
stage, a second instrument and its over-identification test, a weak instrument with its warning
and a confidence set of two rays, and the table of the three fits.

**Dates and times.** `time_between(start, end, unit:)` counts seconds, minutes and hours between
two `DateTime`s and days, weeks, months and years between dates or times; `add_time(d, n,
unit:)` adds days, weeks, months or years; `hour`, `minute` and `second` read the parts of a
`DateTime`; `start_of(d, unit)` gives the start of the day, week, month, quarter or year, and of
the hour. `datetime(x, epoch:)` reads Unix time in seconds or milliseconds, `date(year, month,
day)` builds a date from numbers or columns, and `min` and `max` take a date column, in
`aggregate` too.

**One function with an option.** `bin(x, n:)` cuts a variable into groups of equal size by its
quantiles and `bin(x, breaks:)` at given points; `fill_missing(x, value)` or `fill_missing(x,
method: forward | backward)` fills missing values; `running(x, stat: sum | product)` and
`row(vars, stat: mean | sum)` replace four names; `variance_test(var, group:, method: f | levene
| bartlett)` tests equal variances across groups, Levene's test centred on the median unless
`center: mean` is asked. The names they replace are refused with the line to write instead (see
Breaking changes).

**The language.** A comparison with the literal `NA` is refused with the advice to write
`is_missing(x)` (Z0226). A range of variables, `[educ..age]`, is taken wherever a function takes
several variables. A distribution may be written inside a call: `quantile(dist.t(df: 24),
0.975)`. `print("mean =", x)` writes its plain values on one line, separated by `sep:`. An
option record can carry a variable, `{ se: robust, cluster: vars(firm) }`. Inside a function a
parameter that holds a variable stands for that term in a formula. The type of `rank(x)` and of
`running(x, stat:)` follows the option written, so `rank(x) / n()` checks. `cluster:` takes
every kind of variable `fe:` takes, text and dates among them. `labels(size:, shape:, fill:)`
titles those legends, and the notes of a statement that ends in a plot are those of the plot.

**Messages.** A numeric function given a text, category, date or logical column is refused when
the statement runs, with the conversion that suits the case, where it gave `NA` on every row. A
warning or a note names the variable the script wrote, not a column of the engine's own; what
one statement would say several times is said once with a count; a conversion inside one branch
of `case` counts only the rows that reach the branch; a definition of `aggregate` that reuses a
variable's name is noted when a later definition reads it. Two mistakes that passed `zeff check`
and ended in an internal error are found by the check (a field a result's record does not have;
an index into the table `t.ci()` returns).

**Two commands.** `zeff explain Z0401` prints the page of a code, with what it means, an example
and the way out; alone it lists the codes by family, and `--format json` gives the page to a
program. The language server sends the same page to an editor. `zeff report-bug` writes one file
a user can read and attach to a report: the engine's version and platform, the failing statement
with the statements it depends on, the warnings and the seed of the run, and the data's schema,
never the data unless `--include-data` is written. It then shrinks the failure, removing rows
and statements by halves while the same failure persists, and `--synthetic` replaces the values
of the rows left. Nothing is ever sent. Four templates for reports are in the repository, and
the shrunk reproducer of every corrected fault joins the conformance suite: eight so far.

**Typst.** `export("table.typ")` writes a fit, a report table, a test's result or a figure as
Typst, in the layout of the LaTeX form. The engine sets no PDF itself: the Typst library was
measured as a dependency and is not linked, for its size (7.2 MB compressed in a browser, where
the whole engine is 2.8) and for the licences of some of its hyphenation patterns.

**Help pages.** The pages of `core`, `text` and `time` (94) and those of the ten new functions
carry what every page will carry by 1.0: the commonest forms first, missing values under their
own heading, a caption on every example, a common mistake with the message it really gives, and
the equivalents in R and Stata. The other 145 pages follow at 0.9.0 and 0.10.0.

**Faster, and lighter.** `wilcoxon_test` of two groups no longer stores every pair of
observations: on the shipped wages by sex it needed 662 MB and now peaks at 23 MB, with every
figure the same to the bit. The studentized range far out in its upper tail returns at once,
where one call took 28 seconds; `posthoc` on the 190,757 rows of the review against R now runs
in under a second.

**Corrected.** The probability of a truncated distribution close to its bound kept five digits
where thirteen are right, since 0.3.0; `dist.f` past its median changes in its last digit or
two. `n_missing([x1, x2, x3])` inside `add` gave 0 on every row and is now refused. A regression
whose fixed effects leave no residual degree of freedom ended in an internal error, since 0.6.0,
and is refused with Z0802. See Breaking changes for each.

## Breaking changes

- **Ten names are merged into six**, and each old name is refused with the line that says what
  to write:
  - `days_between(a, b)` and `months_between(a, b)` are `time_between(a, b, unit: day)` and
    `time_between(a, b, unit: month)`.
  - `add_days(d, n)` and `add_months(d, n)` are `add_time(d, n, unit: day)` and `add_time(d, n,
    unit: month)`.
  - `quantile_group(x, 4)` is `bin(x, n: 4)`, and the cut points of `bin` are now given by name,
    `bin(x, breaks: [...])`.
  - `fill_forward(x)` is `fill_missing(x, method: forward)`.
  - `running_sum(x)` and `running_product(x)` are `running(x, stat: sum)` and `running(x, stat:
    product)`.
  - `row_mean(vars)` and `row_sum(vars)` are `row(vars, stat: mean)` and `row(vars, stat: sum)`.
- **A numeric function given a column of a type it does not take stops with Z0401**, where 0.7.0
  gave `NA` on every row without a word: `log(industry)` on a text or category column, a date or
  a logical. The rewrite is to convert first, `to(x, Float)` or `to(x, Int)`, or to remove the
  call. A function of `time` or `text` given such a column ended in an internal error and is
  refused the same way.
- **`x == NA` and `x != NA` are refused** (Z0226); they were true for no row. The rewrite is
  `is_missing(x)` and `not is_missing(x)`.
- **`n_missing([x1, x2, x3])` inside `add` is refused.** It counted the three columns as three
  items and gave 0 on every row, which was wrong. Until `row(vars, stat: n_missing)` arrives
  (open decision #166, 0.9.0) the count across variables is written as a sum of
  `to(is_missing(x), Int)`.
- **`print` of several plain values writes one line.** `print(1, 2)` printed two lines and
  prints `1 2`; one value prints as before, and a frame, a list or a result still prints as its
  own block. A stored output of an older script changes in those lines; there is nothing to
  rewrite.
- **A message is said once.** A warning or a note that one statement gave several times word for
  word is given once with a count, and names the variable the script wrote. The notes of a
  statement that ends in a plot are those of the plot. No result changes; a stored copy of the
  messages is renewed once.
- **A regression whose fixed effects leave no residual degree of freedom is refused** (Z0802).
  It ended in an internal error in 0.6.0 and 0.7.0.
- **Corrected values of two distributions.** A probability or a quantile of `dist.truncated`
  very close to its bound was right to five digits and is right to thirteen; `dist.f` past its
  median may differ in its last digit or two. A stored figure that depended on them is renewed
  once.
- **The new names.** A script's own function called `time_between`, `add_time`, `hour`,
  `minute`, `second`, `start_of`, `ivreg`, `first_stage`, `ar_test` or `variance_test` is
  refused with Z0109; the rewrite is another name for the script's function.
- **The project's pin.** A 0.7.0 project sets `zeff = "0.8.0"` in its `zeff.toml` (Z0710 says so
  otherwise), as at every release until the launcher of 0.9.0.

Changes that only let more scripts run: a range of variables wherever several are taken; a
distribution written inside a call; a variable in an option record; a parameter of a function as
a term of a fit; `cluster:` on a text, date or time variable; `se: hc2`, `se: hc3` and
`influence` with one fixed effect; arithmetic on `rank(x)` and `running(x, stat:)`.

Of the 486 conformance digests of 0.7.0, 470 are the same at 0.8.0. One changed in its values,
`dist/values`, for the two corrections of the distributions above. The other fifteen changed in
the wording or the layout of what is printed and in no figure: seven stored messages and eight
printed pages, most of them the sample projects', where `print` now writes one line, a message
is said once, or a printed summary names a function by its new name.

## Known issues

See `docs/known_issues.md`, whose section for this release has ten entries. The ones a user
meets first: `ivreg` is two-stage least squares, with no LIML or GMM before 1.0, and its
standard errors divide by N − k as R, `fixest` and Stata's `small` option do, where Stata's
default divides by N (open decision #150); the Anderson–Rubin set is for one instrumented
regressor; a clustered first-stage F needs more clusters than instruments; a first stage that
fits exactly prints an F of twenty-one digits where a warning would serve; `export` writes Typst
and the engine sets no PDF from it; 145 help pages are not yet of the fuller form, and the Stata
column of the revised ones has not been run in Stata; a probability below the smallest double
has few digits, which a truncation more than 37 standard deviations out shows (open decision
#173); a warning is said once for a statement, not once for a loop; the gaps the review against
R met (a row number, the length of each text value, one value out of a column, a row-wise count
of missing values, an import that should widen whole numbers to decimals, a coefficient of
-0.0004 printed as 0.000 in `report`) are due at 0.9.0; and no reader outside the project has
yet read its scripts. Resolved at this release: `winsorize`'s double warning; the functions of
`time` and `text` that ended in an internal error on a column of the wrong type; a parameter as
a term of a fit inside a function; `hc2`, `hc3` and `influence` with one fixed effect; the
weekly mutation job's time budget.
