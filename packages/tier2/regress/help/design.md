# design

## Summary
The design a regression was computed on, as a frame

## Description
Gives the columns a fit was computed on: a variable for each term of its table, under the
term's name, with the value the term takes in each row. `m.design()` after
`w.reg(log(wage) ~ educ + industry)` has `(intercept)`, which is 1 in every row, `educ`, and
a variable of 0 and 1 for each industry but the first, as `industry=Mining`.

It makes the model's own arithmetic visible: how a category was coded, what an interaction
or a power is row by row, which rows the fit used. It is also what a diagnostic over the
design is written on: the correlation of two regressors, the leverage of a row against its
values, a plot of the residuals against one column.

The frame has the length of the frame the fit was computed on, as `residuals()`, `fitted()`
and `influence()` have, so its rows are the rows of the data: a row the fit dropped has `NA`
in every variable, and `drop_missing` on any one of them leaves the rows used.

In R this is `model.matrix(m)`. In Stata the columns are generated by the factor-variable
notation and are not kept; `predict` and `estat` use them unseen.

## Arguments
- fit: A regression fit.

## Value
A `Frame` with a Float variable for each term of the fit, in the order of its table, and for
a weighted fit the variable `(weights)`. A name that is not letters, digits and `_` is written
in backticks: ``m.design().mean(`industry=Mining`)``.

## Details
The terms are those of the fit's table, under the names of section 7.2 of the Language
Reference: `(intercept)`; a variable by its name; a category as `variable=level`, one
variable for each level but the first; an interaction as its parts joined by `:`; a power or
a function as it is written, `exper^2`, `log(income)`. A term the fit omitted as collinear
has no estimate and no variable here.

The values are those of the model, not weighted. For a weighted fit the weights it used are
in `(weights)`: analytic and sampling weights as the fit scaled them, to sum to the number of
rows used, and frequency weights as given. A row left out for a weight of zero has `NA`.

With fixed effects (`fe:`) there is no intercept, and each variable is what is left of its
column after the effects are taken out: the column less its means within the groups of the
effects, which is the design the estimates were computed on. Its values are deviations and
not the data. A row dropped as a singleton has `NA`.

The fit keeps the frame it was computed on by its handle, so nothing is copied when a model
is fitted; the design is built again when it is asked for, from the same frame, formula,
weights and effects. The frame stays in memory as long as the fit does.

The least-squares coefficients are recovered from it: regressing the outcome on the
variables of the design, without a second intercept, gives the estimates of the fit.

## Examples
> let w = dataset(wages)
> let m = w.reg(log(wage) ~ educ + exper^2 + union)
> m.design().head(4)
frame · 4 rows × 4 variables

      (intercept)   educ   exper^2   union
  1             1     17        81       0
  2             1     17       100       0
  3             1     17       121       0
  4             1     17       144       0
> m.design().n_rows()
18412
> m.n_obs()
18179
