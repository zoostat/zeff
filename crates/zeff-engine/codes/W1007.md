# W1007 — weak instruments

## Meaning
The excluded instruments explain little of the instrumented regressors once the other regressors are accounted for (section 7.1 of the Language Reference). The two-stage estimate then leans towards the least-squares estimate it was meant to correct, and its standard error understates how uncertain it is. The fit is reported; the warning is printed beside it.

The warning reads the F statistic printed under the fit and warns when it is under 10: the Cragg–Donald F under classical errors, the Kleibergen–Paap rk Wald F under robust or clustered errors. With one instrumented regressor under robust or clustered errors it also reads the effective F of Montiel Olea and Pflueger and warns when that is under its critical value, the one that guards at the 5% level against a bias above a tenth of the worst case. The critical value depends on the data: it is 23.1 with one instrument and lower with several.

## Common causes
- An instrument only loosely related to the regressor it stands in for.
- Many instruments of which one or two do the work: the others add nothing to the first stage and lower its F.
- Several instrumented regressors that the instruments move together, so that each first stage is strong and the instruments still cannot tell the regressors apart; the Cragg–Donald and Kleibergen–Paap statistics measure exactly this.
- Fixed effects or control variables that absorb most of the instrument's variation.

## Example
```
> card.ivreg(lwage ~ exper + expersq + black + south + smsa + educ, instruments: educ ~ nearc4 + nearc2, se: robust)
warning[W1007]: weak instruments: the Kleibergen–Paap F is 9.72, under 10, and the effective F is 9.64, under its critical value of 19.44
  = help: with weak instruments the estimate leans towards the least-squares one and its confidence interval is too narrow; `ar_test()` gives a test and a confidence set that hold however weak the instruments are
```

## Fix
Read `first_stage()` to see which instruments carry the first stage, and drop the ones that carry nothing. Report `ar_test()` beside the estimate: the Anderson–Rubin test and its confidence set are valid whatever the strength of the instruments, where the usual interval is not. A stronger instrument is the only cure for the estimate itself.
