# W0503 — a bracketed sum in a formula

## Meaning
Inside brackets and function calls a formula's operators are arithmetic (section 7.2 of the Language Reference, "Arithmetic"): `(a + b)` is one column, the sum of `a` and `b`, and `(a + b)^2` squares that sum. R reads the same brackets as grouping terms, and `(a + b)^2` as every two-way interaction of `a` and `b`, so a formula carried over from R may not mean what it did. The warning says what Zeff computes; the formula is used as written.

## Common causes
- `y ~ (a + b)^2` from R, meaning the interactions.
- `y ~ (a + b)` with brackets added for clarity.

## Example
```
> let f = y ~ (a + b)^2
warning[W0503]: `(a + b)^2` is a power of the sum `a + b`; R reads it as the interactions of its terms
```

## Fix
For the interactions, `interact([a, b], order: 2)` or `a + b + a:b`; for the terms, `a + b` without brackets; for the square of the sum, nothing: it is what was written.
