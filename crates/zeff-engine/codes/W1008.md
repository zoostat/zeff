# W1008 — fitted probabilities numerically 0 or 1

## Meaning
A binary model converged, and in one row or more its fitted probability is 0 or 1 to the machine's precision: within ten machine epsilons of either, the rule of R's `glm`. The outcomes are not separated (that is the error Z0803, found before the fit), so the maximum of the likelihood exists and the estimates are it; but an observation far out on a regressor is all but decided by the model, adds nothing to the information, and the rows near it carry the estimate. The fit is still reported; the warning gives the count of such rows, prints beside the result and is listed in the footer of a `report` table (section 11.1 of the Language Reference).

## Common causes
- An outlier in a regressor: a value hundreds of times the others, such as an income entered in units where the rest are in thousands.
- A code for a missing value, such as `9999`, left in a regressor and not declared with `na_values:`.
- A regressor that nearly separates the outcomes: all but a few rows on one side of a value have the same outcome.

## Example
```
> let d = import("data/extreme_regressor.csv")
> d.glm(y ~ x + z, family: binomial)
warning[W1008]: 3 fitted probabilities are numerically 0 or 1
  = help: in these rows the regressors all but decide `y`; the estimates exist, but look at the largest and smallest values of each regressor
```

## Fix
Look at the extremes of each regressor, `describe`, and at the rows that hold them; correct or declare a wrong value, or rescale the regressor (its logarithm, or a cap with `winsorize`). If the values are right, the estimates stand, and the warning may be allowed on that line with `// zeff: allow(W1008)` and the reason.
