# W0211 — expected counts below 5 in a chi-squared test

## Meaning
`chi2_test` compares observed counts with the counts expected under the hypothesis; its p-value comes from the chi-squared distribution, which approximates the statistic's distribution well only when every expected count is at least about 5. The test ran, but one or more expected counts are below 5, so the p-value may be off.

## Common causes
- A small sample spread over many values of a variable.
- A cross-table with a sparse row or column: a rare level of one variable.
- Proportions in `p:` that give a small expected count to a rare value.

## Example
```
> frame(a = ["x", "x", "y", "y", "z"], b = [1, 2, 1, 2, 1]).chi2_test(a, b)
chi2_test · independence · a × b
  H0: a and b are independent in the population
  H1: a and b are associated in the population

  a       1   2   Total
  x       1   1       2
  y       1   1       2
  z       1   0       1
  ─────────────────────
  Total   3   2       5

chi2(2) = 0.83   p = 0.659
decision at α = 0.05: fail to reject H0 (p = 0.659)
warning[W0211]: 6 of 6 expected counts are below 5, so the chi-squared approximation may be poor (line 1)
  = help: `fisher_test` is exact for a small table; merging sparse values raises the expected counts
```

## Fix
Use `fisher_test` for a small cross-table, which is exact; or merge sparse values with `recode` so that every expected count reaches 5.
