# Z0421 — more than 5,000 values given to the Shapiro–Wilk test

## Meaning
`shapiro_test` was called on a variable with more than 5,000 non-missing values. Royston's (1995) approximation to the distribution of W, which gives the p-value, was fitted for samples of 3 to 5,000 and is not defined beyond that, so the test stops there, as R's `shapiro.test` does (section 6 of the Language Reference; open decision #91). Rows with `NA` are dropped before the count.

## Common causes
- A survey or panel dataset with tens of thousands of rows.
- A test meant for a subset, written on the whole frame.

## Example
```
> wages.shapiro_test(lwage)
error[Z0421]: `shapiro_test` takes 3 to 5,000 values, but `lwage` has 18,201
  = help: Royston's approximation stops at 5,000, as R's `shapiro.test` does; with this many rows, judge normality from `qq_plot`, or test the distribution with `ks_test`
```

## Fix
Judge normality from `qq_plot`, test the distribution with `ks_test`, or test a subset of at most 5,000 rows chosen with `where`, `head` or `sample`. In a sample this large the test rejects normality for departures too small to matter, so the plot is the better guide.
